\section{Introduction}
MicroInvest is an educational financial DApp for investing beginners. Any connected user can deploy an independent Investment contract, give it a title and explanation, and publish it in a searchable directory. Other users discover a project, open its own detail page, deposit Sepolia test ETH, inspect their shares and redeem principal at any time. The application is available at \href{https://sc6113-developemnt-coursework.onrender.com/}{MicroInvest on Render}.

Each pool retains simple rules: 1 ETH equals 1 internal share, including fractions; no yield, platform fee or share transfer is offered. The creator has no privilege to withdraw other participants' funds. The project demonstrates discovery, custody, ownership and confirmed transactions rather than a real asset strategy.

\section{Problem Statement}
A beginner needs to find the intended project and understand where funds are held, how contributions determine ownership and how principal can be recovered. A contract address alone is hard to recognize. A single unnamed pool also offers no convenient way to choose between independent projects.

MicroInvest associates a public title and explanation with a verified contract and a unique directory ID. Name/keyword search improves discovery; separate contract ledgers isolate participants' positions. Exact share conversion, clear transaction status and accessible history make the contribution and redemption process visible.

\section{Objectives}
The objectives are to provide user-created named pools and keyword discovery; integrate MetaMask with a responsive web interface; implement exact fractional shares and caller-bound redemption; persist project metadata and confirmed activity; and evaluate authorization, error handling, usability and gas. The submission follows the coursework requirements and all 13 report sections \cite{assignment}.

\section{System Architecture}
The browser uses Flask APIs for project search, metadata, pool state, positions and history. MetaMask signs deployments, deposits and redemptions directly on Sepolia. After deployment, the creator signs a separate registration message; Flask verifies the actual creation and recovered signer before publishing metadata. Flask holds no wallet private key.

\section{Smart Contract Design}
Each Investment deploys the unchanged \texttt{MicroInvest.sol} bytecode with its own balance and ledger. \texttt{shares[address]} and \texttt{totalShares} use unsigned integers. One displayed share has $10^{18}$ units, so deposited wei equals credited share units without rounding.

\begin{center}\small
\captionof{table}{State transitions and contract validation}\label{tab:contract}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.29\linewidth}p{0.37\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Transition} & \textbf{Validation} \\
\midrule
\texttt{deposit()} & Credit caller and total by \texttt{msg.value} & Positive deposit \\
\texttt{withdraw(amountWei)} & Debit caller/total; send equal wei to caller & Positive amount within caller shares \\
\texttt{withdrawAll()} & Redeem caller's complete position & Nonzero caller shares \\
Ledger / accounting reads & Return shares, principal, balance and excess & No state change \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
For example, a 0.003 ETH deposit creates 0.003 shares; a 0.001 ETH redemption leaves 0.002. Full exit clears that position. Gas is paid separately and is not reimbursed with principal. Each pool enforces:
\[
\mathrm{totalShares}=\sum_i\mathrm{shares}_i,\qquad
\mathrm{redeemableWei}_i=\mathrm{shares}_i,\qquad
\mathrm{balanceWei}\geq\mathrm{totalShares}.
\]
Authorization follows \texttt{msg.sender}. A creator can participate but cannot redeem anyone else's position. There is no owner, pause, upgrade, transferable token or investment manager. Pool A and Pool B have separate storage even when the same wallet participates in both.

Withdrawals validate entitlement, debit state and then transfer ETH. A reentrancy lock blocks nested calls; failed transfers revert the ledger change. These decisions follow checks-effects-interactions \cite{solidity}. Adversarial receivers test refusal of ETH and attempted nested withdrawal.

\texttt{Deposited} and \texttt{Withdrawn} events identify the investor, amount and resulting shares. Only successful confirmed events enter business history. Pending/reverted transactions and wallet rejections create no successful event row. Forced ETH is excess balance, not new shares or yield.

The optimizer uses 200 runs; operations change a fixed amount of storage without iterating over investors. Historical receipt gas appears in Section 9. Reusing tested bytecode adds independent projects without adding creator privileges or changing principal accounting.

\section{Application Design}
The English interface begins with an Explore directory. Users search names or words from explanations, browse paged results, or filter projects by the connected creator. Matching is literal and case-insensitive; whitespace-separated terms must all match. IDs and addresses distinguish projects even when titles are identical. This is keyword search, not semantic search.

A project opens \path{/investments/<id>} with its title, explanation, contract address, pool metrics, deposit/redemption controls and personal position. Its own activity page preserves project and wallet scope. The detail preview shows the five newest confirmed records; full activity loads 20-row pages and older records. Empty recent block ranges are traversed automatically.

\section{Implementation}
The backend separates \texttt{catalog.py} (metadata/search), \texttt{chain.py} (validated blockchain reads) and \texttt{history.py} (persistent activity synchronization). Templates and browser modules provide Explore, creation, detail and full-history pages. Jinja escaping and \texttt{textContent} keep creator text as data.

Creation has two approvals: MetaMask first deploys the supported artifact; after a successful receipt the creator signs an EIP-191 registration message \cite{signature}. The message binds the title, explanation, creation hash, chain and HTTP origin. Flask checks successful direct creation, exact creation/runtime bytecode and signer equality with the actual deployer. It saves server-derived address/block/creator and returns a unique ID. Identical retries reuse the same entry; conflicting metadata is rejected. A declined registration can resume with the existing creation hash, avoiding a second deployment.

\subsection*{Database history and hosted deployment}
The history API now returns \texttt{source: "database"} and the latest five original-pool records, including the confirmed 0.04 ETH redemption in block 11857372. Completed scan ranges include empty intervals; missing ranges are fetched in windows of at most 5,000 blocks, using RPC chunks of at most 1,000. Exact events are upserted without duplicates, and subsequent queries use the stored coverage plus any newly mined blocks.

The server checks the latest stored end-block hash against the chain. A changed checkpoint invalidates only that wallet/pool index for rebuilding; a hash change during scanning aborts the write. The index improves recovery across application restarts but still requires RPC validation. The UI fills its five-record preview or 20-row history page across empty ranges rather than declaring an empty first range to be the complete history.

\section{Challenges Encountered}
Exact integer wei and string API amounts avoided rounding across Python, Solidity and JavaScript. Selecting the authorized signer explicitly fixed reliance on the first wallet account. Adding projects required every request, position, history page and pending hash to carry its own contract/wallet context. Registration needed deployment verification and signature binding so a participant could not claim another creator's contract.

Old transactions appeared missing when the latest 5,000-block window was empty. Following the next cursor recovered them; persistent event/range tables and automatic older-range loading resolved the presentation issue. Separating live deployment status from actual endpoint verification also exposed stale served code during the hosted update.

\section{Limitations}
Pools hold principal without market allocation, diversification, yield or a guarantee of investment value. One mined confirmation is sufficient for interface feedback, but is not finality. Public RPC and database availability affect reads/discovery; initial historical backfill may require several requests. Literal search is not semantic search, and published titles/explanations cannot be edited or moderated in this version.

Addresses, contract activity and creator text are public; no identity profile or private key is stored. Signed metadata proves the deployer's authorization, not the truth of an investment explanation. Direct MetaMask deployments are supported; factory/smart-contract-wallet creation is outside scope. There is no external security audit, load test, novice-user study, complete accessibility audit or administrative rescue/upgrade path. The prototype is evaluated with test ETH rather than real savings. Render Free PostgreSQL expires 30 days after creation unless upgraded \cite{render}.

\section{Future Improvements}
Future work includes beginner usability studies, moderation and metadata amendment with clear authorization, better search relevance, multiple wallet providers and monitored incremental indexing. Database backups/restore exercises, hosted restart checks and broader reorganization testing would strengthen operational evidence. Any real strategy, transferable token or additional asset requires a separate financial specification and security review.

\section{Conclusion}
MicroInvest now combines independently created named contracts, keyword discovery and project-specific participation with the original exact-share/principal-exit rules. Creator-authorized registration and PostgreSQL retain the directory; confirmed activity is indexed while entitlements remain on-chain. Local tests cover accounting, authorization and isolation, and the updated Render APIs demonstrate live metadata and recovered history. The system meets the coursework's financial DApp learning objectives within its stated educational scope.
